# Product decisions

## Definitions before commands

A complete beginner should not be blamed for vocabulary the course has not supplied. Tools are introduced with what they do, what they do not do, and why they appear in the workflow before the learner is asked to operate them.

## Browser rehearsal plus real desktop work

Low-friction practice makes a first attempt easier, but engineering fluency still requires the terminal, editor, filesystem and Git. Browser sandboxes are an additional feedback path, not a simulation presented as the whole job.

## Evidence before completion

Learn, Practise, Build and Verify remain distinct steps. Completion uses output, reviewed changes, explanations or bounded quizzes rather than an unexplained “done” click.

## AI apprenticeship rather than delegation by default

The product teaches a five-level help ladder: attempt, hint, review, delegate and defend. The learner is gradually allowed more assistance while retaining responsibility for understanding the target, inspecting the change and explaining why it is safe.

## Local-first rather than account-first

The course does not need an account or backend to teach its core curriculum. Removing those systems reduces privacy exposure and operational complexity, but creates an honest device-switch boundary. Validated backups and merge-aware restore make that boundary manageable without pretending it does not exist.

## Calm motivation

The learner is returning to a difficult subject, often after a long gap. The interface uses next actions, evidence and gentle re-entry rather than streak pressure, points, badges or confetti.
